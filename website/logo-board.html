OpenH1 logo board

Five original marks for OpenH1, each shown as a mark, a wordmark lockup, an app icon in the platforms' masks, and a favicon at its true size. Every mark is drawn on the grid of its 16-pixel favicon, so its straight edges land on whole pixels.

The wordmark is Overpass, the site's typeface, drawn as outlines. The colours are the site's: navy and white, the menus' blue, and amber as the one accent. Files are in website/logo/, one folder per concept, made by website/logo/build.py.

01

Ligature

An H and a 1 that share a stem: the 1 grows out of the H's right side, and the amber crossbar is the bridge the port builds from the original game to new machines. It can stand in for the “H1” in the wordmark.

Why it's safe. Pure letterforms of the project's own name, built from rectangles. No character, helmet, ring or lettering from the game or its makers.

02

Source

The 1 between code brackets: a game rebuilt from its source, in the open, by its community.

Why it's safe. Square brackets are a universal programming symbol and the 1 is a plain numeral. Nothing is taken from the game's art, logos or fonts.

03

Readout

A 1 cut into segments at one angle, like a digit on an instrument's readout: precise and technical, with the amber flag as the one lit segment.

Why it's safe. Segment displays are a generic convention of clocks and meters, not the game's HUD. No glyph or interface element is copied.

04

Lock-on

Four corner marks around an amber diamond: a focus frame that has locked on, open at the corners like the project.

Why it's safe. Corner marks and a diamond are generic geometry, used by cameras and editors alike. There is no reticle from the game and no ring.

05

Gateway

A doorway, open at the bottom, with the 1 stepping through it: one game, out to every platform.

Why it's safe. An arch is a common symbol for a portal or a door. It is not a ring, and nothing in it comes from the series.

At small size

Each concept's favicon at its true 16 pixels in a light and a dark browser tab; the bare mark at 16 pixels on white, on dark and in one colour; and the favicon enlarged eight times to show its pixels.

Recommendation: Ligature

Ligature is the one to take forward. It is the only concept that spells the project's name rather than illustrating an idea about it, which makes it the most ownable and the hardest to mistake for anything else. It is three straight-edged shapes on the 16-pixel grid, so its stems and crossbar land on whole pixels in a browser tab, and it reads the same in one colour, on light and on dark. The amber crossbar carries the site's accent without needing a visor or a helmet to get there, and the mark can replace the “H1” in the wordmark for a tighter lockup later.

Runner-up: Source. The clearest “open source” story and very legible small, but brackets around a character are a common pattern for developer tools, so it is less distinctive. Readout and Gateway are good at large sizes but lose detail at 16 pixels; Lock-on is safe but says little about the project.
