OpenH1 logo board, round 2

Ten new concepts in very different styles, several of them in the look of the early-2000s console era: black plastic with a light inside, glowing tubes, chrome box art, CRTs. Each is original, evokes rather than copies, and has a small version drawn by hand for 16 pixels, where the glow and bevel effects drop out.

Each concept is shown in its own palette, plus one-colour versions. Files are in website/logo/round2/, one folder per concept, made by website/logo/round2/build.py. Round 1 is in logo-board.html.

01

2001 hardware: black plastic, inner light

Power Core

A chunk of black console plastic with a recessed well and a lime core glowing inside it in the shape of a 1, like a power light seen through smoked plastic. The era's hardware, as a badge.

Why it's safe. It's a rounded slab with a numeral in it: no character, helmet, ring or anything from the game.

Why it's not the Xbox logo. No X, no sphere or jewel shape. The lime is a glow accent, shifted to yellow-green, on a black brand.

02

2001 dashboard: organic glowing tubes

Tube

H1 runs in liquid-filled tubes with a bright core and a soft bloom, the organic, lit-from-inside feel of the era's console menus, in teal rather than green.

Why it's safe. Monoline letters of the project's name. No interface element, ring or shape from the game or its menus.

Why it's not the Xbox logo. Teal-to-cyan, not the console's green; letters, not an X; no orb or blob logo.

03

2001 box art: chrome, bevel, extrusion

Big Box

Slanted chrome letters with a deep blue extrusion and one lens sparkle, straight off a 2001 game box, with the shelf-edge orange band on the app icon. Loud on purpose; the small version is flat.

Why it's safe. The letters are Overpass, slanted, not the game's logo type, and the box art is our own composition. No characters, no ring, no publisher marks.

Why it's not the Xbox logo. Blue and orange chrome, not green; no X, no orb, no console branding band.

04

Pixel mark on an amber phosphor screen

CRT

H1 and a blinking cursor in fat pixels on an amber monitor: the decompilation, old screens and LAN-party CRTs. The favicon is the pixel art itself, so it's exact at 16 pixels.

Why it's safe. Our own pixel drawing of the name; no game sprite, font or screen is reproduced.

Why it's not the Xbox logo. Amber phosphor, not green; pixels and a cursor, no X and no orb.

05

Military stencil, field plate

Field Stencil

H1 stencilled on a clipped field plate: the stencil bridges break the crossbar and cut the 1's flag loose, and the flag is painted signal orange. A squad marking for a project anyone can join.

Why it's safe. Generic stencil lettering on a plain plate. No eagle, globe, rank insignia, unit badge or any emblem from the series.

Why it's not the Xbox logo. Sand, olive and orange, stencilled letters: nothing of the console's colours, shapes or type.

06

Custom heavy wordmark

Slab

OPENH1 drawn from scratch as one heavy block of letters: big rounded O and P, slab E, N and H, a 1 with a sharp flag. The name is the logo; the H1 in a red tile is the icon.

Why it's safe. Custom letters drawn here for this name, unrelated to any game or console lettering.

Why it's not the Xbox logo. Red and bone, upright custom slab letters: no X, no orb, no green.

07

Abstract geometric

Split

A solid square split open: the cut between its halves is a 1, and the right half has slid up as if the block were being opened. Open, and one, in two shapes.

Why it's safe. Two plain polygons; the 1 exists only as the gap between them. Nothing figurative at all.

Why it's not the Xbox logo. Coral and cream squares with a straight cut: no X, no curves, no orb, no green.

08

Monogram on an original shield

Crest

A notched shield parted down the middle, burgundy and bone, with a varsity H and 1 counterchanged across it inside a gold keyline: a team crest for a community project.

Why it's safe. A heraldic shield of our own outline with collegiate letters. No eagle, globe or insignia from the series, and no ring.

Why it's not the Xbox logo. Burgundy, gold and bone heraldry: none of the console's shapes, colours or type.

09

Kinetic: a trajectory

Launch

A 1 whose flag is a trajectory: a dotted arc rises from the lower left, turns into a swoosh and lands as the thick stem. Motion and arrival: the game, launched somewhere new.

Why it's safe. An open arc that becomes a numeral. It never closes into a circle or ring, and has no figure in it.

Why it's not the Xbox logo. Violet-to-orange heat, an open arc: no X, no sphere, no green.

10

Character built from type

Buddy

A face made of type: two 1s for eyes, their flags as eyebrows, and a sideways dash for a mouth. Cheeky and determined. It stands in for the O of OpenH1, so the wordmark smiles.

Why it's safe. A rounded square and three glyphs. Not a character from any game, and no helmet or visor; the face is bare.

Why it's not the Xbox logo. Sunny yellow and ink, a face of numerals: no X, no orb, no green.

At small size

Each concept's small version at its true 16 pixels in a light and a dark browser tab, on white and on dark, the one-colour mark at 16 pixels, and the small version enlarged eight times.

Top three

Honourable mentions: Split is the most timeless and abstract (two shapes, the 1 is only the gap), Crest and Launch are polished and distinctive, and Field Stencil has the most character at full size. Big Box is the most fun but too loud for a front-page mark; it would make a great release banner or sticker.

No. 1

Power Core

The most 2001-console piece on the board without borrowing anything: black plastic, a lit core, gloss. It is a complete object, so it makes the strongest app icon here, and the small version is a clean lime 1 on black that reads instantly in a tab.

No. 2

CRT

The best story for this project, the decompilation and old screens, with the warmest era feel: LAN parties and amber monitors. Its pixel favicon is exact at 16 pixels, and the bitmap wordmark with its cursor gives the site a voice no other port has.

No. 3

Slab

The boldest brand: the name itself is the logo, drawn from scratch, so it is fully ownable and needs no symbol to explain. Red and bone stand out from every other game project, and the H1 tile is a strong, simple icon at any size.
