Platforms

Native on every machine you play on

One port of the game, built for each system. Keyboard and mouse, controllers, touch or your own two hands.

  • macOS

    A Mac app with its own Metal renderer.

    Apple silicon · macOS 14.4 or later

  • Windows

    A regular Windows program that keeps itself up to date.

    Keyboard and mouse, or a controller

  • Linux

    A portable folder that suits package managers too, and updates itself.

    x86 · x86-64 · 64-bit ARM

  • Steam Deck

    Fills the screen at its own shape, with Steam Input.

    16:10 · up to 90 Hz on the OLED model

  • Android

    Touch controls with gyro aiming, or a controller. Drawn with Vulkan.

    Android 9 or later · 64-bit

  • VR

    Play it in an OpenXR headset, drawn with Vulkan.

    Steam Frame · SteamVR on Linux · Monado

Multiplayer

How game night works

System link, without the cables. Nobody has to set up a server.

  1. Host a game

    Choose Create Game, then Internet. Keep it public to list it in the server browser, or make it private for friends with the link.

  2. Send the link

    The game puts an invite link on your clipboard. Paste it into any chat.

    halo://join/3f9a0c…e71b

  3. Everyone's in

    Friends open the link and join. Any Custom Edition map they're missing downloads from the players in the game.

  • Co-op campaign
  • Up to 128 players
  • 4 split screen on each machine
  • Voice and text chat
  • Server browser
  • Streamer mode

Everyone plays together. Every build runs the same netcode, so players on Mac, Windows, Linux, Android and VR join the same games, and so do players of OpenCE, the community port OpenH1 grew from.

VR

Step inside it, and do it by hand

Made on a Steam Frame. The game plays in an OpenXR headset in stereo, with your head tracked, and your hands do what the buttons did.

Everything VR can do →

  1. Reload by hand. Take a magazine from your hip and push it in.

  2. Draw from holsters. Reach over your shoulder or to your hip for the weapon carried there.

  3. Throw grenades. They land where you threw them, as hard as you threw.

  4. Raise a scope to your eye. Look through the sniper rifle's or the pistol's, magnified.

  5. Drive with your hands. Turn a Warthog's wheel and a Ghost's handlebars.

  6. Flip it back over. Lift an overturned vehicle with both hands.

How it works

Built from the game's own code

OpenH1 compiles the decompiled C source of the Xbox game with few, marked changes, then supplies what the Xbox did: graphics, sound, input, files and networking. Each system gets its own renderer: Metal on the Mac, Vulkan on Android and in VR.

The same results on every machine

Every build does the game's maths the same way, with one shared maths library, so different machines stay in step in a network game.

A new netcode

Each machine moves its own player at once, and the host decides the rest of the game. Read how.

Smooth at any refresh rate

The world runs at 30 ticks a second, as on the Xbox. OpenH1 draws the frames between ticks at your display's rate: 60, 120 or 240 Hz.

    Your copy

    Bring your own game

    OpenH1 includes none of the game's data. You need your own Xbox disc image of Halo: Combat Evolved. Any version works, and players of the European version play with players of the North American one.

    1. Get OpenH1 for your system. Downloads are coming soon; until then, you can build it from the source.

    2. Start the game and choose your disc image (.iso or .xiso) when it asks.

    3. The game copies out its maps once, then you're in.
