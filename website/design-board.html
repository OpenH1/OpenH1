OpenH1 · openh1.com redesign Phase 1: direction October 2026

Three directions for the landing page

Pick one direction (or mix parts of them) before anything gets built. Each has its own audience, look and plan for turning a visitor into someone who stars, watches and shares OpenH1, while there are no downloads to offer yet.

Everything a visitor would read here is checked against the repository (README.md, AGENTS.md and the platform READMEs). The screenshot frames are empty on purpose: we don't have real captures yet, and we won't fake any.

Common ground

What every direction keeps

These rules hold no matter which look you choose.

Rules for the page

  • No download buttons yet. A clear “Downloads coming soon” in their place.
  • No play-in-browser. The web build isn't a feature for now, so it isn't mentioned.
  • No Discord link until OpenH1 has its own server.
  • Static HTML and CSS, almost no JavaScript, fast on a phone, 16px side margins, no sideways scrolling.

Legal distance

  • No Microsoft, Xbox or Halo logos, official art, ring imagery or Xbox green.
  • “Halo: Combat Evolved” only as a plain description of what the port runs.
  • The full disclaimer in every footer: not endorsed or affiliated; Halo is Microsoft's trademark; no game data included.
  • Platforms named in plain text, not with company logos.

The message, in order

  1. It's the 2001 game, running natively on your machine.
  2. It's better than it was: online, co-op, mods, sharper picture.
  3. It's real, open and legal: your own disc, built in the open.
  4. Here's how to follow it until downloads open.

Direction A

A

Spartan HUD

Military sci-fi, as if the page were the game's own menu: dark hull, shield-blue edges, visor-gold actions, readouts in a monospaced font.

For: Halo fans who grew up on the Xbox and PC versions Feels: switching the console on Theme: dark only, on purpose

Why it would convince

A fan decides in three seconds whether something “feels like Halo”. This page answers that before a single screenshot loads: the colours, the type and the HUD details say it's the real game, done with care. Fans star and share what feels like home.

Watch out for

It sits closest to the official look, so it needs restraint: generic sci-fi only (no ring, no glyphs, no Xbox green, no official fonts). Many fan sites look like this, so it earns less trust from the tech crowd. Dark only, because a light HUD reads as a different product.

Direction B

B

Open project, everywhere

A clean, modern open-source project page, the kind made for a well-loved developer tool: generous space, plain facts, a platform table and real numbers.

For: tech and preservation crowd, Mac, Linux and Steam Deck players, press, contributors Feels: calm, precise, trustworthy Theme: light and dark, both designed

Why it would convince

The hard sell isn't excitement (the name does that). It's trust: is this real, is it legal, will it run on my machine? Plain facts, the decompilation's lineage and a visible engineering story win over the people who post links on forums and news sites. Their shares bring the fans.

Watch out for

It's the least emotional of the three, so it leans hard on one great screenshot or clip in the hero. Without one, it can feel like documentation. It also has the most distance from the official look, which is good for legal comfort.

Direction C

C

Game night

Nostalgic 2001 LAN party: chunky wide type, sticker colours, an invite ticket. Dark mode is the basement at 2am; light mode is the photocopied flyer.

For: lapsed players who remember system link, and the friends they'll send it to Feels: warm, loud, social Theme: dark “basement” and light “flyer”

Why it would convince

The strongest memory people have of this game is playing it with friends. The page sells that: “you can play together again, from anywhere, and nobody has to run a server.” It turns every visitor into a recruiter, which matters more than a star when the goal is a full lobby on launch day.

Watch out for

It undersells the platforms and the engineering, and a playful style can look less trustworthy to the tech crowd. It also needs people in the pictures (a lobby, a co-op moment), which we don't have yet.

Recommendation

Build B, dressed in A's colours, with C's game-night section

Use B's structure and voice. The people who decide whether OpenH1 spreads are the ones who share links on forums and news sites, and they reward plain, checkable facts. B also keeps the most distance from the official look, which protects the project.

Give its dark mode A's palette (shield blue, visor gold, Overpass), so a fan still feels the game in it. Many players browse in dark mode, and that is where the game's colours feel most at home.

Borrow C's “How game night works” section for the multiplayer part of the page. Invite links with no servers are the most exciting thing in the project, and C tells that story best.

Main action throughout: Star on GitHub, with “Get release alerts” as the second step. That's honest and needs no sign-up list until you decide you want one.

Your call

Questions for you

  1. Is the helmet icon the final logo? It's close to the game's hero character, which is a legal risk for a front-page mark. An original mark or a wordmark alone would be safer. Do you want options?
  2. Can we get real screenshots and a short clip? Each direction needs them. The list: a campaign moment at 4K with enhanced lights; the Mac app in a window; Steam Deck in hand; Android with touch controls; a view from inside a VR headset; a busy lobby or server browser; a co-op moment. Who can capture them, and are we allowed to show the game's maps?
  3. Is linking to GitHub OK while downloads are “coming soon”? The GitHub README already has a download table, so anyone who clicks “Star on GitHub” will find builds. Should the README hide them too, or is “coming soon” on the site just a soft launch?
  4. Do you want an email sign-up? It's the strongest “notify me”, but it needs a small form handler (on the same Cloudflare account), storage, and a privacy note. Without it, we use GitHub's Watch and the releases feed.
  5. Can we say Mac and VR players join the same games as everyone else? The README only lists Linux, Windows and Android as playing together. If cross-play is tested, it's a headline-worthy claim.
  6. Which renderer is the default on Android and in VR? On master, Android starts with OpenGL ES 3 and the VR release draws with OpenGL; Vulkan is a build option for both. The page currently says “OpenGL or Vulkan” to stay accurate.
  7. Should we name people in the credits? The page thanks OpenCE and the decompilation projects. Do you also want contributors listed, or a link to GitHub's contributor list?
